## 1. 7‑in‑1 RS485 soil sensor (NBL‑S‑TMC‑7) — Modbus decode ✅ high

Read all 7 values in **one** Modbus request, **function 0x03 (holding registers)**,
start `0x0000`, count `0x0007` → 14 data bytes (7 × 16‑bit **big‑endian** words).

| Reg | Param | Scaling | Unit |
|----|----|----|----|
| 0x0000 | Temperature | raw / 10 | °C |
| 0x0001 | Moisture | raw / 10 | % |
| 0x0002 | EC (conductivity) | raw × 1 | **µS/cm** (÷1000 → mS/cm) |
| 0x0003 | pH | raw / 100 | pH |
| 0x0004 | Nitrogen (N) | raw × 1 | mg/kg |
| 0x0005 | Phosphorus (P) | raw × 1 | mg/kg |
| 0x0006 | Potassium (K) | raw × 1 | mg/kg |

Worked example from the manual: `0x0114`=27.6 °C, `0x0146`=32.6 %, `0x004B`=75 µS/cm,
`0x02D2`=7.22 pH. Ranges/accuracy: Temp −40…80 °C (±0.5), Moisture 0–100 % (±5),
EC 0–10000 µS/cm (±10 %), pH 0–10 (±1), NPK 0–2000 mg/kg.
*Source: manufacturer manual (niubol.com). **This corrected a bug in the original
prototype + first firmware draft** which had Temp/Moisture swapped and pH at /10.*

### NPK + pH are LOW‑reliability ✅ high — design around it
- NPK is **not a true nutrient measurement**: the probe measures **EC and
  multiplies by a coefficient** (`ax+b`, default a=1.00, b=0). Manual: "cannot
  accurately measure the actual N/P/K … an empirical, theoretical value … use
  with caution." pH is "reference only" (±1 pH; a glass electrode is needed for
  accuracy). *(niubol manual; comwintop)*
- EC→NPK correlation is **strongly soil‑moisture dependent**: dry soil R²≈0.81,
  **wet soil R²≈0.34** — readings degrade sharply when wet; the probe mainly
  tracks **K**, with weaker N/P sensitivity. *(SAGE Measurement&Control 2023;
  Frontiers in Agronomy 2024)*
- **Build implications:** expose user‑calibratable coefficients; **down‑weight
  NPK when moisture is high**; treat N/P/K (and pH) as **coarse trend
  indicators**, label them "approximate" in the UI, and **do not drive hard
  fertigation decisions from raw probe NPK**. Anchor fertilizer advice on the
  crop‑stage agronomic schedule (§4), not the probe alone.

## 2. YF‑S201 flow meter ✅ high
- **450 pulses / litre, K = 7.5** → `flow(L/min) = frequency(Hz) / 7.5`.
  Accumulate **volume = pulse_count / 450** litres. *(The "22.3 mL/pulse" figure
  was refuted.)* Real accuracy ≈ ±10 %, per‑unit K varies 4.5–7.5 with
  pipe/pressure → **calibrate per unit, use tolerance bands.** *(IJTRD; datasheets)*
- **Leak / no‑flow logic:** valve/pump commanded **ON** but freq ≈ 0 → **no‑flow
  fault** (dry line / failed pump / blockage) → alert + fail‑safe stop. Sustained
  flow with valve commanded **OFF** → **stuck‑valve / leak**.

## 3. Rain‑skip ✅ high
Set the threshold **as small as possible — ~3.2 mm (1/8″) recommended, never
above ~6.4 mm (1/4″)**. Smaller set points save the most water (≈30 % at 3 mm vs
≈17 % at 6 mm in trials). A tipping bucket converts directly to mm (commonly
0.2–0.3 mm/tip — read the shipped sensor's spec); use a **configurable ~3 mm
rolling‑window** rain‑skip. *(UF/IFAS AE221)*

## 4. Crop agronomy by stage (fertilizer schedules) ✅ high
TNAU split‑application NPK (kg/ha) — region‑specific starting references:

| Crop | Basal N:P:K (+organics) | Top‑dress |
|---|---|---|
| Tomato | 75:100:50 + 25 t FYM (+Borax 10, ZnSO₄ 50) | +75 N at 30 d (earthing‑up) |
| Potato | 120:240:120 + 15 t FYM + 60 MgSO₄ (½ basal) | ½ N+K at 30 d |
| Garlic | 40:75:75 + 50 t FYM + 50 MgSO₄ + 1 t neem cake | +35 N at 45 d |
| Chilli | 30:60:30 + 25 t FYM | +30 N at 30, 60, 90 d |
*(TNAU Fertilizer Schedule for Vegetables)*

Nepal sources (NAST / JICA):
- **Onion**: peak marketable yield at **120 kg N/ha** (Agrifound Dark Red, 27.7 t/ha,
  BCR 3.06). Pan‑evaporation scheduling (irrigate at 100 % pan loss, 10‑day gap)
  → 17.2 t/ha — **validates ET/pan‑based scheduling in Nepal**.
- **Cauliflower**: **boron is critical** — 15–20 kg/ha borax basal; peak curd at
  1.3 kg B + 60 kg P₂O₅/ha.
- **Cucurbits** (cucumber): pH **6.0–7.0**, **20–26 °C**.
- **Cabbage**: cool season **7–24 °C**, frost‑tolerant, max yield ≈25 °C.
- **Carrot**: germ 10–15 d (soil ≥10 °C), sow 2 cm deep, 25–30 cm rows, ~100 d.
- **Tomato drip** (NARC, plastic house): **+20 % yield, −30 % water** vs hand
  watering — the headline smart‑irrigation upside. *(NAST 2021; JICA 2016)*
